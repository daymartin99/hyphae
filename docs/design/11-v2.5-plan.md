# 11 · v2.5 — respect the player

v2 made the game completable. v2.5 makes it hold on to people. These come from the act2-1 and fun-1
rounds (Sam, Robin, Morgan) and from David's own play. Agreed with David, Nov 2026.

**Success, measured by the next 5-tester round:** at least 3 of 5 reach Act III; average fun of 3.5
or more; nobody quits because of a bug.

**Ethos, unchanged:** respect the player's time, both on and off. A hidden thing must reliably open.
The voice knows more than it tells, but the controls never hide anything. Failure costs something,
but the player knows the price; a lock is time-limited, never soft. Choose silence over repetition.
Every sound has something visible that goes with it.

## 1 · Time away

- **While-you-were-away card** on return, after more than 2 minutes away. It shows what grew, what
  was spent, and what was lost and why: a default, rot, a tree that failed. It uses the voice's
  register, not a spreadsheet, and gives one line per thing that changed. It shows once, and a tap
  dismisses it.
- **No silent losses offline.** Anything that can cost the player while they're away (a contract
  default, a standing order eating sugar, rot) either is shown on the card or doesn't happen
  offline. If we can't explain a loss, we don't take it.
- **The commuter loop.** A player who opens the app for 30 seconds should always have one
  meaningful thing to do: collect, make one decision, set the next order. NEXT should name it.

## 2 · Say what things are

- **A first-appearance line for each currency:** ∴ minerals, Ψ, spores and labile. The voice says
  once what it is *for* (not what it is), the first time the player sees it.
- **After food vs the sugar sheet:** the two disagree on screen today. Use one definition and one
  number.
- **Tip cost:** a mineral price of "0.00 ∴" reads as broken. Show it to the precision that matters,
  or leave it out.

## 3 · Take the grind out of Act II

- **Early density automation.** Turgor Regulation needs 25 manual DENSER taps today. Bring an
  auto-densify unlock forward to around the third claim, with the standing order's on/off switch
  pattern.
- **Run the economy check** (`standing3.mjs`) before and after; none of this ships unless it holds.

## 4 · A screen that holds still

- **Lists stop re-ordering under the thumb:** sort on open, then hold the order while the player
  is on the tab.
- **Sliders and buttons never move between press and release.**
- **Price ticks stay at 4 s.** Numbers that change on every frame get a fixed width, so the row
  doesn't shuffle.

## 5 · Story: the trees talk to the voice

The trees never speak to the player directly. They speak to the voice, and the voice relays what
they said, in its own words, with its own opinion of them. This adds more communication without
adding a second narrator.

Draft lines below for David to cut and rewrite (≤78 chars, sentence case, Act II):

- The oak asks how long you mean to stay. I told it longer than it.
- The birch wants more than it gives. I said yes. We can afford a liar.
- The elm has stopped asking for anything. That is how you know.
- The beech says the last one like you took everything. I said we are not taking, we are keeping.
- The pines talk among themselves now. They don't lower their voices for me.
- A sapling asked what we are. I said the ground. It seemed to believe me.
- The old oak remembers a fire. It wants to know if we will be the next one.
- The forest asks whether we're staying. Nobody has ever said no.

Also:

- **More Act II lines:** one for each first claim, first tree failure and first standing order,
  and for half and most of the forest held (the last two are done).
- **Show the mushroom.** Fruiting Body puts a visible fruiting body on the plate: a small cap on
  the network, drawn from the same ink. Spores leave it as flecks when they are released.

## 6 · Sound, second pass

- **A soft tick on each voice line,** with the caret pulse as its visible counterpart.
- **Trees:** a low tone when a tree speaks through the voice, and a dull one when a tree fails.
- **Still only at decisions:** pieces stay as they are, playing one at a time, for a few minutes.

## Order

Items 1 to 4 first (they're why people leave). Item 5 runs alongside: David edits the lines while
the build goes on. Item 6 comes last. Then the 5-tester round against the targets above.
