# HYPHAE playtest: Robin (story player), round v2.5-2

## 1. Stop point
I played to **891 game minutes**, away time included, and stopped there because of the **900-minute cap**. I wasn't tired of it. I'd just bought the Fruiting Body, and the Oak Rise patch was sitting there as my next goal.

## 3. What was fun
1. **The trees as characters, and the contracts as a relationship.** The birch nine metres east, then aspen, douglas fir, hemlock, a hollow beech and a dying elm. Each has an age and a reputation. Each ended term gets a line ("the birch remembers that it ended well"). It's story told through a mechanic. I felt I was building trust with neighbours, not clicking "buy minerals". **Story + growth.**
2. **The voice in the log.** It's short, cold and lovely: "you paid for something that was free an hour ago", "wood stored sunlight for sixty million years before anything could eat it", "a mushroom is not the organism. it is the part that is leaving". The adaptation descriptions are worth reading, and so is the log itself. Every purchase felt like it moved the world on a little. **Story + discovery.**
3. **Coming back to a world that had happened without me.** The "while you were away" card had real news in it: "a claim completed", "the floor ran short. you grew at 4% of the pace you left at", and an elm that had died. This game keeps its promises. Dormancy Clause said nothing would default while I slept, and nothing did. **Anticipation + trust.**

## 4. What wasn't fun
1. **"The floor is bare" in the first five minutes.** I tapped, bought tips, and suddenly the main button was greyed out and income had collapsed. The hint ("more is for sale, under grow") did eventually tell me what to do. But for a minute it was exactly "is this a bug or am I missing something?". **Confusion + feeling stuck.**
2. **The Standing Order versus the contracts.** Once I had automatic buying, the AFTER FOOD line read things like "−55.6 sug/s, 0:00 left" in amber. Every contract I tried to sign said "you make 0.00 sug/s … this defaults". The fix was to pause the Standing Order, sign, then turn it back on, every time. That's a chore, and the scary numbers turned out to be harmless, which makes them worse. **Friction + confusion.**
3. **The fiddly phone handling and the scale drift.** Scrolling jumps around. SIGN buttons hide behind the EXTEND button. I twice opened the log by accident when I meant to buy something. Volume sliders are hard to set precisely. And by day two, adaptations cost kilos while I had ten tonnes, so the labile number stopped meaning anything. The only real limit was minerals. **Friction + repetition.**

## 5. The voice and the sound
The voice is the reason I kept playing. Lines that stuck:
- "there is a birch nine metres east. its roots are already on yours."
- "it cannot read you, the tree. it signs anyway."
- "paid for. the beetles were bidding too."
- "Hard little bodies, each one full of a winter you have not had yet."
- "a white stem rises with no leaves. four parts in a hundred now go to it, and always will."
- "the elm is finished. the collateral returns. nothing else does."
- "The oak has been waiting, the way a bank waits."

**Sound:** I never heard any music. Every check said "no music (sparse sound)". The silence suits the forest floor, but nothing ever marked a big moment, like a new patch, a new tree or the elm dying. Waiting felt neutral rather than lovely.

## 6. How far I got
I had five patches: the original, The Second Shadow, The Windthrow Gap, Under the Hemlocks and The Old Coppice. Another "claim" completed while I was away. I had six or seven trees under contract, 59 tips, about 10.8 t of biomass and Fruiting Body. My next goal was Patch: The Oak Rise. The biggest change came around minute 260. The game stopped being "tap, eat the leaves, run out" and became a small economy: buying floor, signing contracts with neighbours, growing into new ground. By the second day it felt like tending a network rather than a mould. The tree roster (hemlock, hollow beech, dying elm) and events like the earthworms made the world feel alive. I didn't see anything as big as a prestige or reset. Autolysis hints at one ("You are allowed to be smaller"), but I never used it.

## 7. Coming back
Every return was a small treat. The "while you were away" card showed big numbers, but what really pulled me in were the one-line news items: "a claim completed. there is another place under you now", "the floor ran short…", "for 21% of that time you were dormant". Going straight to the log to see what had happened felt like reading a diary. The only downside: offline gains dwarf active play. I got about 4 t overnight against tens of kilos while playing, which makes playing feel less important than leaving it alone.

## 8. Would I keep playing tomorrow?
**Yes.** The writing and the trees have me. I want to see the oak, and I want to know what happens if I ever touch the Hollow Beech. It's a soft yes, though. If the next day is just "pause Standing Order, sign, unpause, buy patch", the chores will start to outweigh the story.

## 9. The one change that would make it more fun for me
Make the Standing Order and the contracts work together. Let me tell the Standing Order to keep enough sugar for my contracts, or make the contract coverage count that spending. Then the scary amber numbers and the pause-sign-unpause routine go away and I can spend my train ride reading the forest instead of doing its paperwork. A close second: a sound or music cue when something big happens, like a new tree, a new patch or a death.
