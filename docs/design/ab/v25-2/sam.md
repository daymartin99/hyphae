# HYPHAE: Sam's playtest report (round v2.5-2)

## 1. Stop point
I played to **866 game minutes** (about 14.4 hours on the clock, away time included). I quit just short of the 900-minute cap. I wasn't bored, but I could feel the loop settling: come back, take the "while you were away" pile, buy the next patch, sign contracts, close the app. Around 210 tool calls, so the tool limit wasn't a factor.

## 3. What was fun
1. **Working out the sugar/contract economy myself.** The coverage bar, "you make X; the trees would take Y", collateral that "raises the rate and what the tree will take": it's a small financial sim disguised as ecology. Once I saw that the standing order was the thing starving my contracts, I had a little *aha*. After that, the routine of pausing the order, signing, resuming and closing the app felt like an exploit I had earned. That's mastery and discovering the hidden system, which is exactly what I play these games for.
2. **The forest arriving, one partner at a time.** Birch, then the aspen ("eighty trees with one root"), then two Douglas firs, a hemlock, a hollow beech, a dying elm, an oak on a rise, and a ghost pipe that skims 4% "and always will". Each one was a small surprise with a line of character. Getting more players at the table is the "turns into something bigger" feeling, and it's the best part of the game.
3. **The return screen.** "gone 5 h 30 m … +5.03 t … +7.58 k minerals … a claim completed. there is another place under you now." Every break paid off with a pile and a new patch or tree. That anticipation is what made me actually take breaks.

## 4. What wasn't fun
1. **The default at minute 119.** I lowered the volume, the after-food number said I had room, and I still defaulted. The standing order spends sugar right down to zero, so the payment had nothing to come out of. I lost 120 kg of collateral and reputation, and got "you did not deliver. it will not say so to you. it will say so to them." It's a great line, but it felt *unfair*, because the screen told me I was covered. The "AFTER FOOD" number and the header sug/s disagreed with each other half the time, and I never fully trusted either. That's confusion turned into punishment.
2. **Fighting the layout.** Buttons hide under the fixed log panel and under the floating EXTEND button. Tapping near the log opens the full-screen log sheet. The floor list reorders when you buy, so my "MAX" tap landed on "EAT LATER". SIGN sat under EXTEND, so I had to scroll just right. I lost a few minutes per session to this. Pure friction.
3. **Contracts turned into chores, and the big unlocks were just words.** By hour 8 every session meant opening each tree, dragging volume, dragging term, checking collateral, scrolling, signing, five trees deep. Meanwhile the moments I was most excited for gave me a log line and nothing else: Autolysis (nudged at me for hours, then just a shrink tool), Common Mycorrhizal Network (I hoped the network would *become* something), Fruiting Body. The "open the body" view stayed a thin band of threads at the bottom of an empty screen the whole time. No trees, no patches, no mushroom. It never felt like I was looking at something big.

## 5. The voice and the sound
The voice is the best thing in the game. Lines that stuck:
- "you paid for something that was free an hour ago"
- "other things eat here. that is what a price is."
- "the aspen is not a tree. it is eighty trees with one root."
- "four of them are rooted within a metre of you. none arrived by accident."
- "you did not deliver. it will not say so to you. it will say so to them."
- "the elm is dying and knows the rate for it"
- "a mushroom is not the organism. it is the part that is leaving."

It made the economy feel morally loaded and alive, and that's what made a default sting rather than just annoy. **Sound:** I heard "no music (sparse sound)" for the entire 866 minutes. Nothing ever swelled, not for the first contract, not for tonnes, not for the oak or the fruiting body. Silence suits the tone, but a single musical cue on a new patch or a new partner would have made those moments feel much bigger. Waiting felt exactly like waiting.

## 6. How far I got
I reached about **9.3 tonnes**, with 5 patches (Second Shadow, Windthrow Gap, Under the Hemlocks, Old Coppice, Oak Rise) and 8 tree partners plus the Ghost Pipe. The biggest change came around **minutes 400–520**: Douglas firs and hemlock arrived, I went from kg to tonnes, and the Common Mycorrhizal Network unlocked. It changed in *scale* and *cast*, but the game never became something *different*. The verbs at minute 866 were the same ones I had at minute 30: buy floor, buy tips, buy adaptations, sign contracts. The text keeps hinting at a turn ("at the edges, your threads are reaching for each other. they have not met."), and I kept waiting for it to happen. It didn't, at least not before 900 minutes.

## 7. Coming back
Every return was the best moment of that session. The "while you were away" sheet with big green numbers and a line like "a claim completed. there is another place under you now" made each break feel worth it. Two notes. "the floor ran short. you grew at 2% of the pace you left at" made me feel I'd done it wrong, without telling me how to do it right. And after Dormancy Clause the best strategy was to close the app, which is odd for a game that wants me to tend it.

## 8. Would I keep playing tomorrow?
**Maybe.** The writing and the forest-as-market idea are strong enough that I want to see what the threads "reaching for each other" turn into, and whether the oak is the "patient partner" it promises. But tomorrow would look like today: same verbs, more sliders, bigger numbers. If the next patch doesn't change *what I do*, I'd drift off within a day or two.

## 9. The one change that would make it more fun for me
**Make the network visible and make the big unlocks change the game, not just the numbers.** Let "open the body" show my threads reaching the trees and patches I've claimed. Let Common Mycorrhizal Network or the threads meeting at the edges turn the game into something new, like routing sugar between trees or a map I manage. Let the Fruiting Body actually appear on screen, with a sound. On the practical side: make the contract coverage honest about the standing order, so a default is my fault and not a hidden drain.
