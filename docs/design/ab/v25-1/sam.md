# HYPHAE playtest: Sam (curious), round v2.5-1

## 1. Stop point
I stopped at **837 played minutes** (away time included). That's close to the 900-minute cap, and I also stopped by choice. Every adaptation except one was bought. The last one (Patch: The Oak Rise, 4.4k minerals) was about four hours of mineral income away, so I'd have spent the rest of the cap waiting. I quit, I didn't run out of tool calls.

## 3. What was fun
1. **The birch offering a contract (about minute 8).** Up to then it was a tidy decomposer clicker. Then: "there is a birch nine metres east. its roots are already on yours." A whole new tab, a new currency (∴ minerals), and a negotiation with volume, term, collateral and a coverage bar. This is the Paperclips moment I play these games for: **discovery and a surprise.** The system suddenly had another party in it.
2. **Working out the Standing Order, then coming back to proof (minute 205).** The first away screen said the floor was bare 97% of the time. I reasoned "I need something that buys while I'm gone", saved 15 kg of labile for it and bought Dormancy Clause so contracts couldn't default offline. I came back to +145 kg. **Mastery and optimising.** I diagnosed a hidden constraint and fixed it, and the game confirmed I was right.
3. **The writing as a running log.** "the price went up while you were buying. you did that." / "a mushroom is not the organism. it is the part that is leaving." / "it cannot read you, the tree. it signs anyway." Each purchase got a line that made it feel physical. Tiny bits of **story and mystery** ("at the edges, your threads are reaching for each other. they have not met.") kept me expecting something bigger.

## 4. What wasn't fun
1. **The layout moves under my thumb.** The header grows and shrinks ("owed", "rot" lines), lists reorder themselves (eaten 1st/2nd change), and expanded cards collapse. Over and over I tapped SIGN and opened a different tree, or tapped the Standing Order toggle and opened the log sheet instead. This is **friction**, and it was worst exactly at the moment of making a decision.
2. **Contracts versus the Standing Order is unclear and becomes a chore.** "After food" swung from +3 to −94 sug/s depending on whether the Standing Order was buying, and the runway timer (h:mm, which I first read as m:ss) gave me scares. In the end, every return meant: pause the order, sign contracts, unpause, leave. Then a contract was refused with "you reach, and the reach falls short. (11.3 sug/s at most, 6 seasons)" while my settings were inside both limits. I never worked out why. That's **confusion**, and the repeat ritual is **repetition**.
3. **Patches and big unlocks don't change anything I can see.** "Patch: the second shadow" (90 minerals, my first big goal) gave me one log line and a bigger "it holds" number. The body view stays a pale sketch along the bottom of an empty screen. By the end, at 4 tonnes, it still felt like the same three-tab spreadsheet. **Anticipation without payoff**, and in the last session, waiting with nothing to decide.

## 5. The voice and the sound
The voice is the best thing about the game. Lowercase, dry, a bit ominous, and often funny about economics: "it buys badly and it never sleeps", "a contract with something a hundred years older than you", "signed in sugar. the tree will be watching the seasons", "the good years and the bad years are gone. there is only the average now, and it will not move". It made buying upgrades feel like making choices with a cost, not just bigger numbers. Ghost Pipe ("no chlorophyll and no shame... keeps the four percent") and Perennial Mycelium read like small moral trades, and I liked holding a finger down to take them.

**Sound:** I never heard any music. Every check said "no music (sparse sound)", from minute 0 to minute 837, through new patches, new trees and 4 tonnes. Nothing marked the big moments, so waiting felt like plain silence, not anticipation. A swell when the birch first signs, or when a patch opens, would have made those moments land.

## 6. How far I got
Biomass reached about **4.0 tonnes** (from 0 g). I had five patches (start, Second Shadow, Windthrow Gap, Under the Hemlocks, Old Coppice), six trees in the understory (birch, aspen, two douglas firs, hemlock, hollow beech), and passive minerals from Oxalate Weathering. The biggest *shape* change was early: around minute 8 the bargain tab and mineral economy turned a clicker into a trade game. After that it grew wider, not different. There was no new view, no prestige and no "the network is now the forest" moment, though the text kept hinting at one ("threads reaching for each other"). The next step, Oak Rise ("the way a bank waits"), sounded like it might be the next shift, but it was hours away.

## 7. Coming back
The "while you were away" sheet is good. It's honest ("the floor was bare for 97% of it. nothing grew while it was"), it tells you about seasons and prices, and "a claim completed. there is another place under you now" was lovely. The first returns (+145 kg, +957 kg, +1.3 t) felt really rewarding, because my setup before leaving was what earned them. Later returns were "take it", spend minerals on whatever was next, re-sign contracts, put it down. Worth coming back for the first four times. By the end, less so.

## 8. Would I keep playing tomorrow?
**Maybe.** The writing and the hint that the threads will join keep a mystery alive, and I want to see what the Oak Rise does. But the last two sessions were mostly bookkeeping through a jumpy UI. If tomorrow is the same ritual with bigger numbers, I'll drift away.

## 9. The one change that would make it more fun for me
Make each patch and each tree a **visible, audible event that changes the game's shape**. The body view should show the new ground and the trees you're tied to, and a musical swell should mark the moment. Alongside that, stop the layout jumping, so the mid-game decisions (contracts and the Standing Order) feel like strategy, not a fight with the screen.
