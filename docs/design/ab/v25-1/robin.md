# HYPHAE playtest: Robin (story player)

## 1. Stop point
897 played minutes. The 900-minute cap stopped me, not boredom. I'd have opened it again on the train home. About 150 tool calls.

Average: 3.5.

## 3. What was fun
1. **The log, line by line.** Story. Every purchase comes back as a short, slightly melancholy sentence: "a mushroom is not the organism. it is the part that is leaving." "the good years and the bad years are gone. there is only the average now, and it will not move." "the smell of rain on dry ground is something announcing it survived." I bought things partly just to read what came next. That is the hook for someone like me.
2. **The trees as characters.** Mystery and story. "there is a birch nine metres east. its roots are already on yours." Then an aspen, a Douglas fir, a 220-year-old hemlock. "four of them are rooted within a metre of you. none arrived by accident." It felt like a neighbourhood slowly introducing itself. "it cannot read you, the tree. it signs anyway" made the contracts feel like relationships, not sums.
3. **Coming back to growth and the upgrade names.** A sense of growth and anticipation. Going from grams to 25 kg to 1 tonne to 4 tonnes across real breaks, then spending it on things named Dormancy Clause ("sleep does not break a promise"), Standing Order ("it buys badly and it never sleeps, and you will take that trade") and Ghost Pipe Compact. Each one read like a small decision about what kind of creature I was becoming.

## 4. What wasn't fun
1. **SIGN refusing for no reason I could see.** Confusion and "is this a bug?". The coverage bar said "room to spare" in green. I pressed SIGN and got "the dark holds still. something is missing. (8.52 sug/s at most, 4 seasons)" and then "the market shrugs. you cannot cover it." The poetry doesn't help when I'm stuck. Only by trial and error did I find that the collateral I'd added was the problem.
2. **The standing order quietly draining me.** Unfairness and confusion. After I bought it, sugar sat at almost zero for hours, at -400 or even -900 sug/s whenever I sold something, and nothing told me why. The away screens made it worse. They said "the floor was bare for 97% of it. nothing grew while it was," yet showed +22 kg and +49 kg. Which one is true?
3. **Fiddly bargain screen and a crowded page.** Friction. The header (biomass, labile, sugar, minerals, after food, owed) takes half the screen, so everything else scrolls in a tiny strip. Slider drags often scrolled the page instead. Taps aimed at a button sometimes opened the full-screen log. Collapsed tree cards, three sliders and a checkbox are a lot for the train. I also couldn't tell what a "season" or a "term" was in real time. One contract seemed to end much earlier than I expected.

## 5. The voice and the sound
The voice is the best thing in the game and the reason my score never dropped below 3. Lines that stuck with me:
- "you paid for something that was free an hour ago"
- "paid for. the beetles were bidding too."
- "phosphorus. it left a rock forty years ago and a root this morning."
- "a second place. the same rules, six metres away."
- "at the edges, your threads are reaching for each other. they have not met."
- "a white stem rises with no leaves. four parts in a hundred now go to it, and always will."

Sound: I heard "no music (sparse sound)" the whole time, from the first tap to minute 897. A forest floor with no sound fits, but the big moments (the first tree contract, a new patch, Perennial Mycelium) felt quieter than they should. A swell of music there would have made them feel bigger. Waiting had no sound either, so it felt like plain waiting rather than the forest getting on with things.

## 6. How far I got
4.19 tonnes, four patches (start, Second Shadow, Windthrow Gap, Under the Hemlocks), contracts with birch, aspen, Douglas fir and hemlock, plus Perennial Mycelium and Ghost Pipe Compact. The biggest change came at about minute 3, when the floor went bare and the game turned from "tap to grow" into a market where I bought leaf litter with sugar. The next came around minute 5, when the birch appeared and it became about bargaining with trees for minerals. After that it stayed the same game, just bigger numbers and more trees. The "body" view stayed one branching shape at the bottom of an empty screen. I never saw the new patches or the trees I was trading with, and that undercut the promise of "a new patch, heavy with wood".

## 7. Coming back
Every return opened a "while you were away" card with big green numbers, which feels good. But it said "autumn passed. leaf litter is on the floor at…" every single time, whether I'd been gone 35 minutes or 4.5 hours, and twice it contradicted itself ("nothing grew", then +49 kg). Sugar was usually capped and rotting, which felt like being told off for having a job. The best return was after Dormancy Clause: knowing the contracts would hold while I slept made leaving feel safe. It was always worth coming back, mainly to spend and to read the new lines.

## 8. Would I keep playing tomorrow?
**Maybe, leaning yes.** The writing and the slowly growing cast of trees have me curious ("the old coppice, cut a hundred years ago by someone who did not write it down"). But I'm close to the point where the bargain screen and the unexplained sugar drain make me feel stupid rather than curious. One more "the market shrugs" with no reason and I'd stop.

## 9. The one change that would make it more fun for me
When something blocks me, tell me plainly which number is the problem, in one line under the button: for example "collateral too high: you have X, need Y" or "the standing order is spending 400 sug/s". Keep the poetic line in the log, but give me the plain reason too. That alone would turn my frustration back into curiosity.
