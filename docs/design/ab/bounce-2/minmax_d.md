# HYPHAE: Morgan's playtest report

## 1. Stop point
**61 played minutes. I stopped because I hit the tool-call limit (~270 of 280), not the 6-hour cap, and not because I wanted to quit.** I had just bought "Patch: The Second Shadow" (12 kg labile + 90 minerals) and I wanted to see what the second patch did. I was hooked but also irritated, and I came close to quitting twice (see the timeline).

## 2. How far I got
- Biomass went from 0 g to 236 kg. Rate went from 0.05 g/s to about 150 g/s. I had 39 hyphal tips, three food types, two trees I could trade with (birch, then aspen), 7 or 8 adaptations, and a Standing Order automating purchases.
- The interface changed scale several times. What began as a single button (EXTEND) became a three-tab economy: **grow** (tips plus a floor market with live prices, a "fair" price line and order-of-eating controls), **learn** (an adaptation tree), and **bargain** (sugar-for-minerals contracts with trees, with volume, term, collateral and an exclusive flag). The biggest shift came around minute 14–15. Tips started costing **minerals (∴)** as well as biomass, so the game stopped being "buy generators" and became "run a supply chain". Sugar paid for food, food made biomass, and trees turned sugar into minerals, which gated the tips.
- The second big change was **Sclerotia** at minute 23, which raised the sugar cap from 1.4k to 8.7k. That turned a game where I checked every minute into one where I could put the phone down for 3–5 minutes.
- At the end, **Patch: The Second Shadow** said "a second patch of floor darkens at the edge of the map. it is yours to spread into." I didn't see any UI change before I had to stop.

## 3. Timeline
- **0 min**: One button, "the forest floor is warm / it is waiting for you." Tapped about 100 times to reach 60 g for the first tip. Fine.
- **1 min**: First tip gave +3 g/s of litter eaten, about 1.5 g/s biomass. Payback was under a minute, so I bought every tip I could. Classic and satisfying.
- **3 min** ◆ Sugar appeared, along with "there are two thousand grams of it. you have used ten." I realised the floor was a *finite stock* and my generators were eating through it. Good hook.
- **4 min** ✦ The floor became a market. "the price went up while you were buying. you did that." Price impact in an idle game! Then "you paid for something that was free an hour ago." I laughed.
- **5 min** ❓ Winter arrived. The rate dropped from about 11 to 4 and I couldn't see why until I found the YEAR card (moisture 0.41 ×0.25, warmth 0.55×) at the very bottom of a long scroll. Once I found it I could reason about it: litter on the floor gives cover, cover raises moisture, moisture is the multiplier. That was a good discovery.
- **9–13 min** ⚠ I put the phone down for 4 minutes, which is how I play. I came back to "the floor is bare. it will not stay bare." Biomass gain had fallen to 0.65 g/s, sugar was at the cap and rotting, and the 4 minutes were mostly wasted. The game punished exactly the kind of wait I like.
- **14 min** ◆ Minerals ∴ appeared: "the tips are thinning. something rooted nearby has what they are made of." The tip cost jumped and gained a mineral component. Rhizomorph Cords (+18%, about 105 s payback) was an easy buy.
- **15 min** ✦ I signed my first birch contract. "a root touches a thread and the terms are set." Paying sugar for phosphorus felt like a real mycorrhizal deal.
- **15–22 min** ⚠ This was the treadmill. Food drained at 100–240 g/s and the sugar cap was about 1.3k, so I had to restock the floor roughly every 60 seconds. Worse, the header grew and shrank by a line whenever sugar hit the cap ("rot"), which moved every button about 23 px. Half my purchase taps missed or landed on the wrong control. This is where I nearly quit.
- **19 min** ✦ The Mycelial Ledger added a "fair 0.19 sug/g" line under each price. That gave me real buy-low information, which is exactly my kind of thing.
- **23 min** ✦ Sclerotia took the sugar cap from 1.39k to 8.71k. It changed how the game felt and I could breathe again. "silence is the answer. the tree understands silence." (I had declined a tree's counter-offer.)
- **29 min** ✦ Standing Order: "it buys badly and it never sleeps, and you will take that trade. (Automated, 8% worse than you)". Honest automation with the cost stated. I loved it.
- **28–41 min** ⚠❓ The worst part. I tried five or six larger contracts. Each time I got flavour text ("signed in sugar", "the tree agrees to feed you. it expects something back", "it cannot read you, the tree. it signs anyway"), but **no contract appeared and no minerals came in**. Small contracts (term 1, about 1–2 sug/s) worked. Large ones failed silently. Minerals were *the* bottleneck, so this cost me about 15 minutes of progress, and I still don't know the rule. Separately, "the oak declines" appeared in red, and I never saw an oak.
- **42 min** ◆⚠ Autolysis: "You are allowed to be smaller. It is faster than patience, and it costs what it looks like. HOLD TO TAKE." No numbers. I held it to learn what it did. It dissolved 8 of my tips into labile (+34 kg), which I couldn't spend because I was mineral-gated. The text was honest in hindsight ("costs what it looks like"), but it was a one-way decision I couldn't evaluate first.
- **48 min** ✦ An aspen appeared: "the aspen is not a tree. it is eighty trees with one root." It had a much bigger volume cap and worked first try.
- **49–55 min** ⚠ I left the phone for 5.5 minutes with the Standing Order active and came back to a bare floor again ("the tips find nothing to take. the market is still there."). Rate was 2.6 g/s with 8.5k sugar sitting there. So the automation doesn't actually let you walk away.
- **61 min** ◆ I bought the second patch: "a second patch of floor darkens at the edge of the map." Then I had to stop.

## 4. Mystery vs clarity
**Mystery score: 4/5.** Understanding the economy *didn't* spoil the intrigue. The log kept reframing the mechanics: "a mushroom is not the organism. it is the part that is leaving." "wood stored sunlight for sixty million years before anything could eat it." "somewhere below the leaves, a point begins to wander" (twice, and never explained, which I liked). Each new tab arrived with a line that made the mechanic feel like biology rather than a spreadsheet. The upcoming items (Perennial Mycelium: "You stop having years"; A Gift of Phosphorus: "+4 reputation, one tree"; Forward Contracts) promised more depth. I lost a point because some mystery was really missing information (see below).

**Clarity score: 2/5.** Numbers were mostly excellent: rates, payback times in the "next" line, the fair price, sug/g, "17:49 to empty". The friction was in the interface and the hidden rules:
- **The layout moves under your thumb.** The sticky header (Biomass, Labile, Sugar, rot line, Minerals, After food, owed) plus the log takes the top 450 px. The usable scroll window is about 300 px tall, and it moves whenever the rot line or "X to empty" appears or disappears. Lists also reorder ("eaten 1st/2nd/3rd" shifts as stocks change). I mis-tapped constantly.
- **Contract signing fails silently.** SIGN shows success-sounding prose and then nothing happens. The "coverage" bar (red, orange, green, partial) is never explained. A line like "1.89 sug/s at most" appeared sometimes and not other times. One-contract-at-a-time ("no room in the ground for more") was only implied.
- **"After food" vs the sugar rate.** Both are shown and they often disagree (+9.49 sug/s next to −0.39 after food, "1:22 to empty"). I couldn't tell which one would bankrupt a contract.
- **Autolysis was irreversible and gave no numbers.**
- Collateral is "held, not spent", but my labile never visibly dropped when I set it.

**The difference.** *Intended mystery:* the voice, the wandering point, the oak, the aspen-as-eighty-trees, "the market does not ask what for", what the second patch will become. These made me want to continue. *Broken or unclear interface:* the moving layout, silent contract failures, the unexplained coverage bar, and the two conflicting sugar rates. Those didn't feel like secrets. They felt like the game wasn't telling me a rule it was enforcing, and for me that's the "opaque numbers I can't reason about" that makes me quit.

## 5. The voice
Lines that stuck:
- "you paid for something that was free an hour ago"
- "the price went up while you were buying. you did that."
- "silence is the answer. the tree understands silence."
- "the aspen is not a tree. it is eighty trees with one root."
- "a mushroom is not the organism. it is the part that is leaving."
- "it buys badly and it never sleeps, and you will take that trade."
- "winter is not a pause. it is the same process at a quarter speed, in the dark."
- "tips are cheap. the ground they open is not."

It helped much more than it hurt. Several lines were real hints in disguise: "the floor wants cover", "deadfall: more sugar than leaf, less water held", "the tips are thinning. something rooted nearby has what they are made of". It misled me once: on failed contracts it said "the tree agrees to feed you" when nothing had been agreed. I heard it as the forest itself, or the mycelium's own slow inner narration, dry and a bit amused at me. It never felt like a tutorial voice, and I liked that.

## 6. The single change that would make me play more hours
**Make the floor survive the player's absence and make contracts tell the truth.** In practice: give the Standing Order a target stock or price ceiling that actually keeps the floor stocked over a 5–10 minute absence, show its real behaviour (kg/s bought, why it stopped), and have SIGN either succeed or say exactly which limit blocked it. Even better, fix the sticky header so the controls stop moving. I want waits sized to my next purchase. Right now every wait over a minute ends with "the floor is bare", and the main lever for growth (minerals) fails without explanation.
