# HYPHAE — Sam's playtest report

## 1. Stop point
**333 played minutes. I quit.** Not the cap and not the tool limit, though I was close to the tool limit. I had claimed all six patches and bought every adaptation, ending with Forward Contracts. The adaptations panel then said "nothing to change yet". Minerals and sugar kept piling up with nothing to spend them on. The map still showed the same single fan of threads. The two hooks I'd been carrying for hours (the mushroom line and the oak) never paid off. When I can't see what the game wants next, I put it down.

## 2. How far I got
1.75 tonnes of biomass, year 15, 6/6 patches, contracts with seven trees (birch, aspen, two Douglas firs, hemlock, hollow beech, dying elm), and six foods on the floor (leaf litter, needle mat, fine deadfall, bark slough, fallen log, heartwood stump).

The game did change into something different, twice:
- **~minute 5:** it went from a clicker (EXTEND, one number) to an economy. Sugar appeared, the floor turned into a market priced in sugar per gram, and I could set an eating order (EAT SOONER/LATER). It became a resource puzzle about keeping the floor damp.
- **~minute 12:** the big one. "The Understory" appeared: trees with ages and reputation, and a contract screen with volume, term, collateral and an "exclusive" box. From then on the real currency was minerals (∴), and the game was about negotiating with trees. Patches (more ground) were the long ladder.

Nothing after the trees felt as big a change. Patches added capacity and new food types, but the screen stayed the same.

## 3. Timeline
- **0 min** ✦ "the forest floor is warm / it is waiting for you." One button. Exactly my kind of opening.
- **1 min** ◆ "there are two thousand grams of it. you have used ten." I could already see the scale coming.
- **3–5 min** ✦ Hyphal tips, labile, and then sugar and a market. "other things eat here. that is what a price is." "you paid for something that was free an hour ago." Lovely.
- **12 min** ✦◆ Reflex Arc (hold to extend), then the birch contract. "it cannot read you, the tree. it signs anyway." The game opened right up.
- **19–28 min** ❓⚠ Signing failed silently. I tried a bigger birch contract four or five times. Each time the log printed a cheerful line ("signed in sugar. the tree will be watching the seasons", "the tree agrees to feed you", "you wrap a root…") but no contract appeared, and no collateral or sugar moved. I nearly stopped here. It felt broken, not mysterious.
- **27 min** ◆ "the tips are thinning. something rooted nearby has what they are made of." A good hint that minerals come from trees.
- **43 min** ⚠ Came back from a break to "you have eaten everything you were standing on." EXTEND was greyed out ("the floor is bare"). I'd outgrown my food and minerals were trickling at 0.01/s.
- **44 min** ❓ Worked out by trial and error that low-volume contracts go through and high ones don't. After that, SIGN was greyed out with a red "coverage" bar, which helped, but only some of the time.
- **45 min** ✦ "you let it lie. the rain will do some of the work." EAT LATER on leaf litter keeps the floor damp. A nice "aha".
- **66 min** ⚠ I bought 10 tips without noticing they now cost minerals, and burned most of my scarce minerals. That was my fault, but the "· 1.00 ∴" is tiny.
- **88 min** ◆ "the aspen is not a tree. it is eighty trees with one root." New trees appearing while I was away was the best kind of idle surprise.
- **101 min** ❓ Patch "CLAIM" printed "a clearing, and now it is yours" but cost nothing and changed nothing I could see. I then had to buy the same patch again as an adaptation. Two steps, never explained.
- **102 min** ✦ "+ a second place. the same rules, six metres away." Capacities doubled and bark slough appeared.
- **129–157 min** ✦ Douglas fir, hemlock ("in permanent shade and has never been in a hurry"), and the hollow beech. Contracts with a 4–8 season term paid hundreds, then thousands, of minerals. This was the best stretch.
- **183 min** ◆ Ghost Pipe Compact: "Monotropa has no chlorophyll and no shame. It takes sugar, buys trust, keeps the four percent." I bought it and never understood what it did.
- **245 min** ✦ Biomass reached 1.12 t. ◆ Bought "The Hollow Beech" (4k sugar). "It is enormous and it is empty and it will pay almost anything. Read the term length twice." Then: "the beech pays at once and pays well. somewhere in the terms, a long time is waiting." I never saw the catch. An intriguing set-up with no payoff I could find.
- **276 min** ✦ "the elm is dying and knows the rate for it." The dying elm offered far better rates. Lovely writing married to a real mechanic.
- **308 min** ◆ Patch 6: "The oak has been waiting, the way a bank waits." Then "the rise comes into view, and the oak on it… a patient partner." The oak never appeared in my tree list.
- **333 min** ⚠ Nothing left to buy, "nothing to change yet", and the last message was a warning. I quit.

## 4. Mystery vs clarity

**Mystery score: 4/5.** Knowing the controls never spoiled the intrigue, because the voice kept recasting the mechanics. The market wasn't "a shop", it was "other things eat here. that is what a price is." Contracts weren't upgrades, they were deals with things a hundred years older than me. New trees arriving while I was away (aspen, fir, hemlock, beech, dying elm) kept the world bigger than my understanding of it. I lose a point because several mysteries were set up and never landed for me:
- "a mushroom is not the organism. it is the part that is leaving." I was sure this was foreshadowing fruiting or a prestige, and nothing came of it.
- The oak declined offers for 200 years, then was promised as a partner, and never showed up.
- The Hollow Beech warns me to "read the term length twice", but I could never find the term to read.

**Clarity score: 2/5.** I nearly always knew what I *could* tap. I often didn't know whether it had worked or what it did. The worst friction:
- **Silent contract failure.** SIGN looked enabled, the log printed a success-sounding line, and nothing happened. It cost me about 25 minutes and nearly made me quit. The coverage bar later helped, but at one point it was red and SIGN still looked tappable.
- **CLAIM vs buying the patch.** Claiming does something invisible; you then pay for the patch in Adaptations. The patch card's label lagged behind ("4/6" stayed on screen after I'd bought patch 4).
- **What collateral, "exclusive" and Ghost Pipe actually do.** The contract card explains collateral in one sentence. "Exclusive" and Ghost Pipe are never explained.
- **Standing Order** seemed to stop while I was away and then spend hundreds of sugar per second the moment I came back. That once took my "after food" negative with "0:33 to empty" while I owed contract payments.
- **Navigation.** One very long scroll, a sticky header that covers about the top 40% of the screen, and sliders inside the scroll area. I kept dragging sliders by accident, and taps under the header went nowhere. With seven trees, renewing contracts was a slog of scroll, open, drag, scroll, sign. By the third hour this was the main chore.
- **Tips quietly starting to cost minerals** ("· 1.00 ∴" in small grey type).

**The difference.** Intended mystery: what the trees want, why the floor dries, what ∴ is for. The game *hinted* at all three, and each answer felt earned ("leaf, bark and branch together. each one opens the next."; "you let it lie. the rain will do some of the work."). Broken or unclear interface: silent sign failures, the two-step patch claim, the lagging patch label, controls hidden under the header, and any action whose only feedback was a poetic line that may or may not mean it worked. Too often the log's poetry stood in for a receipt, so I couldn't tell flavour from confirmation.

## 5. The voice
Lines that stuck:
- "other things eat here. that is what a price is."
- "you paid for something that was free an hour ago"
- "wood stored sunlight for sixty million years before anything could eat it."
- "a mushroom is not the organism. it is the part that is leaving."
- "the price went up while you were buying. you did that."
- "leaf litter changes hands. hands is a generous word here."
- "the aspen is not a tree. it is eighty trees with one root."
- "the oak declines. it has been declining offers for two hundred years."
- "a tree ring is a year in which nothing happened fast enough to notice."
- "a log takes thirty years to disappear and is never once empty."
- "the elm is dying and knows the rate for it"
- "You stop having years. The average is very good, and nothing will ever beat the average again."

It mostly helped: it taught the systems by telling me how the forest worked. It misled me twice. Success-sounding lines appeared for contracts that never started, and the patch CLAIM said "now it is yours" when it wasn't yet. It annoyed me only through repetition; "further than yesterday. the roots do not notice yet" and "each press is a millimetre" came round a lot. I heard it as the forest floor itself, or a dry, amused naturalist narrating the fungus in second person. The bracketed "[ reflex arc ]" lines read like the fungus's own ledger.

## 6. The single change that would make me play more hours
**Give every action a plain, honest receipt, and give the endgame a visible next horizon.** In practice the most important piece is the receipt: when SIGN or CLAIM fails or only half-happens, say so in plain words ("the birch won't take more than 2.5 sug/s right now"), and keep the poetry for flavour. After the sixth patch, show *something* on the horizon. The mushroom ("the part that is leaving") is right there in the text. If I'd seen a fruiting or spore option greyed out at 6/6, I'd have played another evening.
