# HYPHAE: Robin's playtest report

## 1. Stop point
**268 played minutes. I quit.** I wasn't capped and I hadn't run out of tool calls. Over four evening sessions with tea breaks between them, I claimed the sixth and last patch, and the Adaptations list said "nothing to change yet". Most of the log was then lines I'd already read. The last new line ("one more mouth in the dark.") came from buying 64 tips at once, and the only reward for that was a bigger number. Biomass was in tonnes, sugar was rotting at -298/s, and there was nothing left to discover. That's when I put a book down too.

## 2. How far I got
The biggest change was **one tap becoming a forest economy**. It started as one button and a weight in grams. Then came the Floor, a list of litter with EAT SOONER / EAT LATER. Then a sugar market with live prices ("the beetles were bidding too"). Then the Understory, where I drew up contracts with named trees: volume, term and collateral sliders, a reputation dot-track, and ACCEPT/DECLINE offers. After that came Patches (1/6 to 6/6), an automatic buyer, a parasite plant taking 4% of everything, and a "Perennial Mycelium" adaptation that flattens the seasons into an average. Units moved from g to kg to t, and the branching drawing at the top filled the header.

The *interface* never changed shape after about minute 32, when the Understory and Adaptations appeared. The scroll just got longer. The tone changed once. "Perennial Mycelium" (~min 191) was the closest the game came to a turn: "the good years and the bad years are gone. there is only the average now, and it will not move." I hoped finishing the patches would open something else. It didn't. 6/6 shows a dash.

## 4. Mystery vs clarity
**Mystery score: 3/5.** For the first ~40 minutes and in each new adaptation card, the writing kept a strong sense of something hidden: the trees as characters with ages and memories, the beetles bidding, the ghost pipe, the unnamed coppice-cutter. Understanding the controls didn't spoil that, because the controls are dry economic machinery and the mystery lived in the prose beside them. What spoiled it was **repetition**. Once the same lines came back and I'd seen every system, the texture went flat. I was also never sure there was a *story* underneath, only a mood. "the same rules, six metres away" is a beautiful line that also admits there is no destination.

**Clarity score: 2/5.** The worst friction:
- **Half the game is hidden below the fold.** I played 30 minutes before finding the Understory and Adaptations, and nothing told me to scroll.
- **Tap targets move.** Buying re-sorts the floor list and the log grows above, so my next tap lands on EAT LATER, or on nothing. A tap after scrolling often just stops the scroll.
- **Too many rates without labels.** SUGAR +1.41 sug/s next to AFTER FOOD -2.01 sug/s next to "0:09 to empty" while sugar was rising. I never fully understood what "after food" or "to empty" measured.
- **The coverage bar's red/amber/green** has no legend, and "collateral" was never explained in play.
- **CLAIM didn't deduct the resources it seemed to cost.** The separate "Patch:" adaptations did, and I never understood how the two relate.
- **ACCEPT on a tree's offer did nothing visible.**
- **The Standing Order** quietly ran sugar at -400 to -650/s with no visible control to throttle it.

**The difference.** Intended mystery: who is speaking the bulleted lines versus the unbulleted encyclopedic ones, what the trees are, the human who "did not write it down", what "a long time is waiting" means in the beech's terms. Those felt deliberate and good. Broken or unclear interface: hidden sections, list items jumping under my thumb, the vanished ACCEPT, numbers that contradicted each other, and the birch/sugar direction mismatch in "the tree sends down the first sweetness" (it reads like a bug in the fiction, not a riddle).

## 5. The voice
Lines that stuck:
- "something under you is already dead"
- "each press is a millimetre. the forest is measured in them."
- "a mushroom is not the organism. it is the part that is leaving."
- "other things eat here. that is what a price is."
- "paid for. the beetles were bidding too."
- "leaf litter changes hands. hands is a generous word here." (Funny the first time, wearing by the fourth.)
- "it cannot read you, the tree. it signs anyway."
- "a small gift, correctly timed, to something that cannot count."
- "the good years and the bad years are gone. there is only the average now, and it will not move."
- "Monotropa has no chlorophyll and no shame."
- "the elm is finished. the collateral returns. nothing else does."
- "a tree ring is a year in which nothing happened fast enough to notice."

Who was speaking? The bulleted second-person voice felt like the forest itself narrating to the fungus: dry, slightly mocking, economist and naturalist at once. The unbulleted lines ("wood stored sunlight…", "the largest organism ever measured is a fungus…") felt like a field guide, a calmer, more factual voice from outside. Mostly the voice **helped**. It made every upgrade worth reading and gave the trees personalities. It **misled** me twice (the birch "sends down sweetness" when I was the one paying, and "the tree agrees to feed you" in the same situation). It **annoyed** me once recycling started: by the third hour, maybe 70% of the log was lines I'd already read.

## 6. The single change that would make me play more hours
**Give the late game new writing and somewhere to go after 6/6.** Make finishing the patches unlock a real shift. The Old Coppice's unnamed person, the beech's "long time waiting", the dying elm and the ghost pipe are already set up as threads, so pay them off as events. Stop the log recycling generic market lines. I'd happily do the number work if the forest kept saying something new.
