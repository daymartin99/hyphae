# HYPHAE — Robin's playtest notes

## 1. Stop point
**271 played minutes. I quit.** I wasn't capped and I didn't run out of tool calls. Around the four-hour mark the learn tab was empty except for Autolysis, which I'd already tried. The last unlock, Perennial Mycelium, told me "the good years and the bad years are gone. there is only the average now, and it will not move." After that every line in the log was one I'd already read. The game had pretty much told me the story was over, and then it kept going. That's exactly when I stop.

## 2. How far I got
In biomass terms I went from 0 g to 3.5 tonnes. I had 121 hyphal tips, six kinds of food on the floor, five patches of ground (the first one, the Second Shadow, the Windthrow Gap, Under the Hemlocks, the Old Coppice, the Oak Rise) and eight trees to bargain with (birch, aspen, two douglas firs, hemlock, hollow beech, dying elm and the oak).

The interface changed shape three times, and those were the real shifts:
- **About 4 min:** a single EXTEND button turned into a dashboard with labile, sugar and a floor you can eat.
- **About 7 min:** the **bargain** tab arrived ("there is a birch nine metres east. its roots are already on yours."). From then on it was a game about contracts, collateral and reputation. That was the moment it became something different: a fungus learning to be a market.
- **About 11 min:** the floor ran out and I had to *buy* litter ("you paid for something that was free an hour ago"). The economy turned into the theme.

After that the scale got bigger: patches, stumps, the oak "waiting the way a bank waits". The interface itself never changed again. I saw the "open the body" view and the mycelium drawing fill the window, but I never got a map, even though the text says "a second patch of floor darkens at the edge of the map."

## 3. Timeline
- **0 min** ◆ "the forest floor is warm / it is waiting for you." A perfect cold open. Who is "you", and who is waiting?
- **1 min** ◆ "something under you is already dead / it comes apart in water." The idea of eating the dead lands straight away.
- **4 min** ✦ "other things eat here. that is what a price is." That's when I knew this was a game about economics wearing a fungus costume, and I was in.
- **7 min** ✦◆ Bargain tab, the birch, and then an unbulleted line in a different voice: "wood stored sunlight for sixty million years before anything could eat it." It paid off at 71 min when Peroxidase unlocked fallen logs.
- **8 min** ❓ I signed a contract with a red "coverage" bar and still don't know what red meant. Then the text said "the tree agrees to feed you" while the numbers said *I* was paying the tree sugar. Who feeds whom?
- **11–17 min** ✦ The floor ran bare. "you paid for something that was free an hour ago." "the price went up while you were buying. you did that." The best stretch of the game.
- **23 min** ◆ "autumn again. the same litter, the same price, and you are larger." A year had gone by and I felt it.
- **31 min** ✦ "a mushroom is not the organism. it is the part that is leaving." I said it out loud.
- **37–60 min** ❓⚠ The treadmill: tips eat the floor, I buy the floor, repeat. The layout kept moving under my thumb, so taps bought the wrong thing, reordered my litter (I seem to have hit EAT LATER by accident), opened the log sheet, or switched tabs.
- **58 min** ✦ "paid for. the beetles were bidding too."
- **65 min** ✦ I found the full log sheet by accident. It's lovely, but it also showed me how much repetition there was.
- **71 min** ◆ "[ patch: the second shadow ]". I didn't know I'd bought it. A new patch "at the edge of the map", but there was no map.
- **79 min** Standing Order (automation). Relief. "It buys badly and it never sleeps, and you will take that trade."
- **104 min** ◆ Autolysis, "(a decision) You are allowed to be smaller." I braced for a prestige reset, held it, and it just converted sugar into labile. Anticlimactic. ❓
- **104–140 min** ◆ The forest fills up: "four of them are rooted within a metre of you. none arrived by accident." The hemlock "has never been in a hurry". "the trees pass your name root to root."
- **165 min** ✦ Ghost Pipe Compact: "Monotropa has no chlorophyll and no shame. It takes sugar, buys trust, keeps the four percent." Then "a white stem rises with no leaves." Brilliant.
- **182–193 min** ◆ The moral slide: "Push harder and they take more, because by now they cannot not." "taken as offered. the forest keeps receipts in its rings."
- **224 min** ◆ The Hollow Beech: "Read the term length twice." "somewhere in the terms, a long time is waiting." The aspen's ask doubled to 112 sug/s and I declined. That thread never seemed to resolve.
- **225 min** ⚠ Perennial Mycelium. "there is only the average now, and it will not move." Haunting, and also the end of anything new.
- **226–271 min** ⚠ Nothing left to unlock. The log only repeated itself. A few fresh facts ("a tree ring is a year in which nothing happened fast enough to notice") weren't enough. I quit.

## 4. Mystery vs clarity

**Mystery score: 4/5.** For the first three hours, understanding the mechanics *deepened* the intrigue instead of spoiling it. Once I realised I was a market and not a mushroom, every card description read as satire: Forward Contracts "says so, in your own exudate", the Two-Sided Book, the Ghost Pipe broker. The two voices kept me reading. The bulleted log is the fungus's present tense. The unbulleted lines ("roots do not find water…", "a forest floor is not a floor. it is the top of something.") feel like a field guide or a dead naturalist leaning in. I lost a point because the big promised mysteries didn't land. The "map" never appeared. Autolysis, labelled "a decision", turned out to be a converter. The Hollow Beech's "long time is waiting" didn't seem to come due while I watched. Perennial Mycelium ends the mystery on purpose, and the game carries on anyway.

**Clarity score: 2/5.** I usually knew *what* I could do, but often not what had just happened.
- **Worst friction, by far:** the layout reflows. New stat rows (Minerals, After Food, rot) appear, cards expand, headers slide in, so a button moves between my look and my tap. I bought the wrong patch, reordered my food, opened the log, opened a biomass explainer and switched tabs, all by accident. ACCEPT on a tree offer missed twice because a header pushed it down.
- Buttons hiding under the floating EXTEND bar (the Mycelial Ledger, SIGN).
- Contract numbers: red, amber and green coverage bars with no explanation. "you pay sug/s, you get ∴/s" while the prose says the tree feeds *me*. "2:24 to empty" with 4.7k sugar banked. "no tree is paying" when I was the one paying. Rep sometimes dropped when I accepted a renewal.
- An offer vanished ("the soil does not give. not yet.") and I couldn't tell whether I'd declined it or it had expired.
- "not enough. the ground stays as it is." is a beautiful way to say "can't afford", but I couldn't always tell which purchase it was answering.

**The difference.** *Intended mystery:* who the speakers are; the ∴ symbol before it got named "minerals"; what the trees really want; the Hollow Beech's terms; the unbulleted naturalist voice. *Broken or unclear interface:* the reflowing layout, taps landing on the wrong card, unexplained coverage colours, the sugar/minerals direction contradicting the prose, a "map" the text mentions and the screen never shows, and Autolysis promising "a decision" and then acting like a button.

## 5. The voice
Lines that stuck:
- "other things eat here. that is what a price is."
- "you paid for something that was free an hour ago"
- "paid for. the beetles were bidding too."
- "a mushroom is not the organism. it is the part that is leaving."
- "needle mat changes hands. hands is a generous word here."
- "Hard little bodies, each one full of a winter you have not had yet."
- "Monotropa has no chlorophyll and no shame. It takes sugar, buys trust, keeps the four percent."
- "taken as offered. the forest keeps receipts in its rings."
- "The oak has been waiting, the way a bank waits."
- "the good years and the bad years are gone. there is only the average now, and it will not move."

It mostly helped. It's the reason I played four and a half hours of what is, mechanically, a buy-low treadmill. It misled me twice: it said the tree feeds me while the screen said I pay the tree, and "map" referred to nothing I could see. It annoyed me late on. "you have eaten everything you were standing on" and "nothing left underneath you" each came round half a dozen times, and that cheapened them.

Who's speaking? I read two voices. The `·`/`>` lines are second person, the fungus's own nervous system narrating to itself, or perhaps the forest speaking to it. The unbulleted lines read like a naturalist's notebook, or the soil remembering. The card descriptions are a third voice, a sardonic broker or the market itself ("you will take that trade"). By the end I suspected the market was the real narrator and the fungus was its customer.

## 6. The single change that would make me play more hours
**Give the post-Perennial game a new act, not a longer average.** For example, make the Hollow Beech's "long time" actually come due, or turn Autolysis into a real fruiting/leaving (the mushroom "is the part that is leaving"), so there's a fresh voice, new log lines and a reason to read again. Until then, stop re-serving old log lines. Silence would be better than repetition. (A close second: freeze the layout so taps land where I aimed.)
