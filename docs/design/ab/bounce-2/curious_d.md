# HYPHAE: Sam's playtest report

## 1. Stop point
**305 played minutes. I quit.** It wasn't the cap or the tool limit (about 230 calls used). I'd claimed all six patches and bought every adaptation. The only thing left on the learn tab was Autolysis, which I'd already taken twice, and it turned out to be a small repeatable conversion, not the reveal I'd hoped for. I'd been told "the oak on it, a patient partner" and the oak never showed up among the trees on the bargain tab. Then the log started handing me lines from my first five minutes again ("somewhere below the leaves, a point begins to wander"). Minerals were piling up at 44k with nothing to spend them on except ever-pricier tips. That's the point where I stop. I couldn't tell what the game wanted from me next.

## 2. How far I got
I went from 0.00 g to 3.2 tonnes. There were three real changes of scale:
- **Minute 4: the floor goes bare.** The free leaf litter runs out and "the market is still there". Suddenly I was buying food with sugar. It was the first time the game turned into something other than a clicker, and it's the best twist in the game.
- **Minute 5: the bargain tab and the trees.** A birch nine metres east, then aspen, Douglas firs, a 220-year-old hemlock, a hollow beech, a dying elm. Contracts have volume, term and collateral sliders, plus a coverage bar. The game changed from foraging to finance. As an accountant I liked that idea a lot.
- **Minutes 90–250: patches.** Second Shadow, Windthrow Gap, Under the Hemlocks, Old Coppice, Oak Rise. Each one unlocks a new food (bark slough, logs, stumps, carrion from a deer that "stopped moving forty metres east").

After that the interface never changed again. It was the same three tabs (grow / learn / bargain) and the same header, just with bigger numbers. "Open the body" always showed the same pale drawing of branching threads. Nothing like Paperclips' jump to space ever happened, and that's where I was hoping it would go.

## 3. Timeline
- **0 min**: "the forest floor is warm / it is waiting for you." Tapping EXTEND. "each press is a millimetre. the forest is measured in them." ✦ Lovely start.
- **1 min**: First tip. Tabs appear. "it grows while you are not looking at it." ✦
- **2 min**: Sugar appears. "you have been making this the whole time." ◆
- **4 min**: Floor is bare. "the tips find nothing to take. the market is still there." Buying litter: "you paid for something that was free an hour ago." ✦ Best line in the game.
- **5 min**: The bargain tab opens on its own. A birch with volume, term and collateral sliders, and a "∴" I didn't understand. ◆❓
- **8 min**: "paid for. the beetles were bidding too." ✦
- **21 min**: My first contract silently fails. All I got was a red log line, "the oak declines. it has been declining offers for two hundred years." I'd signed with a birch, so who's the oak? Second try at lower volume worked. ❓⚠
- **22–24 min**: Taps keep missing. The header grows, the list reorders when I press EAT SOONER/LATER, EXTEND slides under the tab bar, and I accidentally open the log twice and buy Reflex Arc and Antifreeze when I meant something else. ⚠ This is where I nearly put it down.
- **39–53 min**: Dormancy Clause ("while you sleep, the contracts hold") and Standing Order ("it buys badly and it never sleeps") take the babysitting away. Relief.
- **90 min**: "Claim" patch: "the worms were here first and will be here after." ✦ But the patch card still said 1/6 and my minerals weren't deducted. Later I found the actual patch purchase on the learn tab. ❓
- **134 min**: Autolysis, "a decision… HOLD TO TAKE". It fired on a short 0.8 s hold. "part of you dissolves on command now." ◆ It sounded like a prestige reset. It wasn't.
- **160 min**: Ghost Pipe Compact: "a white stem rises with no leaves. four parts in a hundred now go to it, and always will." ◆ Genuinely unsettling, in a good way.
- **186 min**: Common Mycorrhizal Network: "the trees pass your name root to root." ✦
- **216 min**: 1.2 tonnes. "the largest organism ever measured is a fungus, and it is mostly water." ✦
- **245 min**: "+ something large stopped moving, forty metres east." Five minutes later the learn tab offers "The Deer in the Gully". ✦◆ The best setup and payoff in the game.
- **249 min**: Oak Rise patch: "+1 patch, +oak". Learn offers "The Hollow Beech: it will pay almost anything. Read the term length twice." ◆ I took the warning and didn't buy it.
- **279 min**: 6/6 patches. "mycelium does not have a front. every part of it is the front." Then nothing new.
- **305 min**: No oak partner, recycled log lines, 44k minerals with nowhere to go. ⚠ Quit.

## 4. Mystery vs clarity
**Mystery score: 4/5.** Understanding the controls never spoiled the intrigue, because the log carries the mystery, not the mechanics. Knowing that ∴ meant minerals didn't explain "the oak declines" or the white stem that takes four percent forever. The deer is the model: a strange line first, then an unlock that reframes it. The mystery only collapsed at the very end. The oak was promised twice and never came, and once the lines started repeating, the voice felt like a shuffled deck rather than someone watching me.

**Clarity score: 2/5.** I usually knew *roughly* what I could do. What I couldn't trust was that a tap would land on what I aimed at. The worst friction:
- **The interface moves under your finger.** The sticky header grows as resources appear (biomass, then labile, sugar, minerals, after-food, owed) until it fills half the screen. EAT SOONER/LATER reorders the floor cards. Lists jump. EXTEND gets pushed under the tab bar. I mis-bought at least three things this way.
- **Contracts fail silently.** The SIGN button gives no "declined" message. There's only a red log line naming a different tree. I had to guess that a red coverage bar means "won't be accepted".
- **The coverage bar is unexplained.** Is it collateral, or affordability? The colours (green, amber, red) do more work than the label.
- **Patches live in two places.** CLAIM in the grow tab and "Patch: …" items in learn. The grow card showed 1/6, then 4/6 after I'd bought five, with a slowly filling bar and the old cost still printed.
- **"to empty" timers contradict the numbers.** "5:26 to empty" sat next to a sugar rate of −6/s and 9.5k in the bank.
- **Hold-to-take fired on a short hold** for something labelled "a decision". There was no confirmation.
- **The sugar sheet** ("only eaten from the floor moves it") was the only explanation panel I found, and it explained nothing about AFTER FOOD.

**The difference.** Intended mystery: ∴ with no name at first, "the oak declines", Ghost Pipe, the hollow beech warning, the deer, "a forest floor is not a floor. it is the top of something." All of those made me lean in. Broken or unclear interface: shifting layout and misfires, silent contract rejection, the duplicate and lagging patch display, contradictory timers, and the "edge of the map" line when there is no map anywhere. Those made me feel I'd misunderstood the *phone*, not the *forest*, and that's the wrong kind of confusion.

## 5. The voice
The lines that stuck:
- "you paid for something that was free an hour ago"
- "paid for. the beetles were bidding too."
- "fine deadfall changes hands. hands is a generous word here."
- "the price went up while you were buying. you did that."
- "the birch takes what you offer. it does not ask what you are."
- "it cannot read you, the tree. it signs anyway."
- "a white stem rises with no leaves. four parts in a hundred now go to it, and always will."
- "the trees pass your name root to root."
- "the largest organism ever measured is a fungus, and it is mostly water."
- "something large stopped moving, forty metres east."
- "mycelium does not have a front. every part of it is the front."

The voice helped far more than it misled. It's the reason I played five hours. It reads like a dry, slightly amused narrator of the forest's economy, half naturalist and half auditor, talking to me in the second person. It misled me twice: "the oak declines" when I'd offered to a birch, and "the edge of the map" when there's no map. It also annoyed me once it started repeating itself. There's one odd inversion as well. I pay sugar to the tree, yet the line says "the tree sends down the first sweetness", which is the real biology but the opposite of what my numbers did.

## 6. The single change that would make me play more hours
**Give me the oak, and with it a real change of interface after the sixth patch.** The game spends five hours promising that the oak is the big, old, patient partner ("declining offers for two hundred years", "waits the way a bank waits"), and then never delivers it. Make the oak the next act: a bargain that changes the rules, or a body view that becomes a map of the patches I've spread into. Then I'd have a reason to keep going instead of watching 44k minerals pile up. (Close second: freeze the layout. Pin the header to a fixed size and stop lists reordering under my finger.)
