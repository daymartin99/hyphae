# HYPHAE: Robin's playtest report

## 1. Stop point
I stopped at **406 played minutes**, about fourteen minutes short of the 7-hour cap. I quit on my own. In the second act the story stopped moving. In my last ten minutes not one new line appeared. I was still short of the next thing I could buy (Differentiation, 1.22k of the 1.4k Σ it costs). The tab had said "11 D of differentiation unspent" for an hour, and I never found anywhere to spend it. I'd also more or less reached the cap.

## 3. What was fun
1. **The voice, and wondering whose it is.** The log is the game for me. There are at least three registers. The lowercase "·" lines read like the fungus's own interior monologue. Unbulleted asides ("winter is not a pause. it is the same process at a quarter speed, in the dark.") feel like a narrator or a field guide. After DECIDE, the lines become capitalised full sentences, which feels like a new mind speaking. I kept reading the log panel just to see who was talking. That was **mystery and story**, and it's exactly why I play Disco Elysium.
2. **DECIDE: "Stop tasting. Start knowing."** Four hours of hints paid off at once: threads reaching for each other and not meeting, then anastomosis, then the action potential, then "something is gathering. 11:34", then a decision that "does not undo." I held the button, the screen filled with STOP TASTING / START KNOWING, and for the first time I had music. The game turned into something else. That was **anticipation and surprise**. It felt like my first moment in Outer Wilds.
3. **Contracts with trees who have personalities.** The birch "takes what you offer. it does not ask what you are." The aspen remembered that I'd failed it. The hollow beech came with "read the term length twice", and the oak "signs. it does not do this often." The Ghost Pipe broker "keeps the four percent." Even the adaptation names reward a reader: Mycelial Ledger, The Two-Sided Book, Forward Contracts "it says so, in your own exudate." That was **story through mechanics**: the economy reads like a quiet moral fable about debt.

## 4. What wasn't fun
1. **Opaque refusals and silent costs.** Trees turned me down with "the root turns away. it is not the season. (50.4 sug/s at most, 7 seasons)" and "the thread stops short. it needs more sugar behind it", even when my offer was below the stated maximum. I never learned what I'd done wrong. Tips MAX also spent ~150 minerals I'd been saving for a patch, with no warning that tips cost minerals at all. That's **confusion and unfairness**.
2. **The interface fights me.** Cards slide under the sticky log panel. The SIGN and EXTEND buttons sit under the tab bar, so I tapped "learn" when I meant to sign. Lists reorder while I'm reading them, and sliders are hard to set to small values. I spent far more taps scrolling than deciding. That's **friction**.
3. **The middle chores and the late grind.** In act one, the floor went bare every ten minutes and I had to restock it, then re-sign every contract one slider at a time. In act two it was DENSER x10, ADVANCE, wait for the signal cap, while "N D of differentiation unspent" sat there with no way to spend it. That's **repetition, and waiting with nothing to decide**. When nothing new is being written, that's where I lose interest.

## 5. The voice and the sound
The voice is the reason I kept going. Lines that stuck:
- "a mushroom is not the organism. it is the part that is leaving."
- "wood stored sunlight for sixty million years before anything could eat it."
- "paid for. the beetles were bidding too."
- "you did not deliver. it will not say so to you. it will say so to them."
- "a forest floor is not a floor. it is the top of something."
- "a hypha grows only at the tip. everything behind the tip is plumbing."
- "Perennial Mycelium: You stop having years. The average is very good, and nothing will ever beat the average again." I refused to buy it on principle.
- "There is a road. Eleven centimetres of dead mineral, and absolute."
- "Colonisation starts. The stand will not notice until it is too late."

The sound: for five hours there was nothing ("no music, sparse sound"). That silence suited the forest floor, and it made the DECIDE piece land enormously: lo-fi solo keys at 76 bpm, then a groove, then a breakdown with no kick. The log even wrote it into the fiction: "Somewhere under the network, slow keys begin. Something is keeping time." The music made that moment feel huge. Then it ended after about three minutes, and I never heard music again. I wish it had stayed as the sound of the new mind. It would have made waiting in act two feel like listening instead of just waiting.

## 6. How far I got
I got through all of act one: six patches (the Second Shadow, the Windthrow Gap, Under the Hemlocks, the Old Coppice, the Oak Rise), the hollow beech, the fruiting body, anastomosis and the action potential. Then I took **DECIDE at about minute 298**, and the game became something different. Biomass jumped from 6 t to 50+ t. Sugar and the seasons gave way to Signal (Σ) and a capped "Mind". The voice switched to capitalised sentences, and a hex map appeared with the goal "consumed 0% of the forest, 1/61 stands". Rivals (Phellinus) and the road showed up. By the end I held 9 stands and had consumed 1% of the forest. It was a real turn, both tonal and mechanical: the patient partner of the trees became something that consumes the forest. That reversal is chilling and good.

## 7. Would I keep playing tomorrow?
**Maybe.** The DECIDE turn and the last few lines suggest a darker second half I want to read: the road, the rival fungus, the forest that "will not notice until it is too late". But the last hour was densify, advance, wait, with an "unspent" counter I couldn't use and long silences in the log. I'd open it tomorrow for one session to see whether Differentiation brings the voice back. If the next hour is also just raising a cap, I'll stop.

## 8. The one change that would make it more fun for me
**Keep the log talking during the grinds, and make every refusal say exactly what's missing.** One new line from the forest, the trees or the rival every few minutes of waiting, and a plain explanation whenever a tree says no, would turn the dead stretches into reading time. That's what this game does best.
