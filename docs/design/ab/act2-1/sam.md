# HYPHAE playtest: Sam, 41, accountant

## 1. Stop point
I stopped at **352 played minutes** because I hit the **tool-call limit** (about 450 calls). I didn't choose to quit. I had just worked out what "DENSER" does in the new forest phase, and a log line said something called Armillaria is "growing toward you from the north." I'd have kept going.

## 3. What was fun
1. **DECIDE and the reveal (minute 305).** After five hours of a quiet, music-less ledger, holding a button labelled "(a decision)" took over the screen, music started ("decide piece, lo-fi, 76 bpm, keys alone"), and the game turned out to have a second, bigger game under it. It was the Universal Paperclips moment I'd been promised: a **surprise** that pays off the patience, plus a **sense of growth**, from grams of leaf litter to a mind spread over 61 forest stands.
2. **The bargain screen and the sugar ledger.** Contracts with trees have volume, term, collateral, exclusivity and coverage, and "you pay / you get / over term" sits right there. Tapping SUGAR shows "comes from / goes to / net". That's my day job made poetic, and it scratched the **optimising** itch. The trees have ages and reputations, which gives the numbers a bit of story ("a contract with something a hundred years older than you").
3. **The log as a story.** I read every line, and the writing rewards that: "a hypha grows only at the tip. everything behind the tip is plumbing." "a forest floor is not a floor. it is the top of something." "The oak has been waiting, the way a bank waits." It made long waits feel like **anticipation**, not dead time, because I knew the next line would be worth reading. It's a **mystery** too: who is "buying on its own", what is coming "from the north".

## 4. What wasn't fun
1. **The first 15 minutes.** One EXTEND button, numbers in milligrams, and words I didn't know (labile, minerals, the floor). The useful "+1" tip button only appeared after I'd extended a while and scrolled. "The floor is bare" stalled me several times with no clear fix. That's **confusion and feeling lost**. A Dark Room gives you a sentence and one obvious thing to do. This felt hidden rather than revealed.
2. **Contract sliders.** Every bargain meant dragging volume, term and collateral sliders, watching "coverage" go red, and getting refusals like "the thread stops short. it needs more sugar behind it" or "not enough. the ground stays as it is." I re-did slider maths about ten times, some of it at screen scroll positions where sliders were half off-screen. That's **friction**, and the refusals read as arbitrary until I learned the rules.
3. **The forest-phase wall (minutes 310–350).** Every MIND upgrade cost more than my cap ("more than you can hold"), the status line said "7 D of differentiation unspent" and I never found where to spend D, and "consumed 0%" didn't move. The log kept dropping riddles ("The price is higher than your ceiling. The ceiling can move.") while I hunted. That's **confusion and feeling lost**, at exactly the moment the game had won me back. I also saw a raw template leak in the log: **"{region}. Something there is willing to talk."**, which broke the spell.

## 5. The voice and the sound
The voice is the main reason I kept going. It's spare, a bit wry, and rewards close reading. Lines that stuck:
- "somewhere below you, something starts buying on its own. it pays a little too much and never stops."
- "a hypha grows only at the tip. everything behind the tip is plumbing."
- "claimed. the worms were here first and will be here after."
- "Ghost Pipe Compact: Monotropa has no chlorophyll and no shame. It takes sugar, buys trust, keeps the four percent."
- "the elm is dying and knows the rate for it" / "the elm is finished. the collateral returns. nothing else does."
- "Perennial Mycelium: You stop having years. The average is very good, and nothing will ever beat the average again."
- "Something moved from one end of you to the other, and it was not food." / "it is still doing it. there is nothing to do with it."

Sound: for the first ~305 minutes there was **no music**, only "sparse sound". Then DECIDE brought in a 3-minute lo-fi piece ("keys alone... the groove... breakdown, no kick... fading out"), which made the moment feel enormous. The silence before it made the music land. The cost is that the silence made the long waits feel lonelier. Some quiet ambient bed during waits wouldn't hurt.

## 6. How far I got
Floor eating → hyphal tips → contracts with birch, aspen, firs, hemlock, hollow beech, oak and a dying elm → patches (Second Shadow, Windthrow Gap, Under the Hemlocks, Old Coppice, Oak Rise) → Autolysis → Anastomosis → Action Potential → **DECIDE**. The biggest change was at **minute 305**: after DECIDE it **became a different game**. The labile/sugar/minerals economy gave way to a map with hexes, FOREST/MIND tabs, 61 stands to colonise ("advance", "DENSER"), signal Σ with a ceiling, and "differentiation" points. Biomass went from 7.8 t to 8.9 kt in about 45 minutes. I'd colonised 4 of 61 stands and just learned DENSER raises the signal cap (412 → 559).

## 7. Would I keep playing tomorrow?
**Yes.** The DECIDE reveal earned a lot of trust, Armillaria is coming from the north, and I'd just found the key (DENSER) to the wall I hit. I want to see what 61 stands and "consumed %" lead to. The forest phase needs to stop hiding its main verb, though. If I'd hit another 30 minutes of "more than you can hold" with no clue, I'd have stopped.

## 8. The one change that would make it more fun for me
**Make the next verb findable without riddles.** When something blocks me ("the floor is bare", "more than you can hold", "7 D unspent", a contract refusal), the "next" line should point at the control that fixes it (for example, "floor bare: buy litter", "ceiling: press DENSER on a stand", "spend D in ..."). Keep the poetry in the log. Let the "next" line be the plain-speaking ledger clerk.
