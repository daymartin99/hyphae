# HYPHAE: Robin's playtest

## 1. Stop point
I stopped at **346 played minutes**, by choice. I didn't hit the cap or the tool limit. Learn said "nothing to change yet". I put the phone down for half an hour, came back, and found not one new line in the log. All that was left was minerals and sugar going up with nothing to spend them on, and sugar rotting at its cap. My friend said "stick with it", and I did. It paid me back handsomely for about four hours, and then it went quiet.

## 3. What was fun
1. **Working out what the trees wanted, from the prose alone.** The bargain screen just says "you get ∴/s". Then the log said "the tips are thinning. something rooted nearby has what they are made of." That one line explained the system. Mystery solved by reading closely is exactly why I play Outer Wilds.
2. **The understory as a cast of characters.** The birch "does not ask what you are". The oak "does not do this often". The Hollow Beech is "enormous and it is empty and it will pay almost anything". The dying elm pays the best rate in the forest because it "knows the rate for it". Then: "the elm is finished. the collateral returns. nothing else does." I felt that one. Every trade had a story behind it, which kept the number-management bearable. That's anticipation and mystery: who arrives next, and what do they want?
3. **The named patches and the foreshadowing that paid off.** At about 30 minutes it said "a mushroom is not the organism. it is the part that is leaving." Four hours later I could buy a Fruiting Body: "for one night in the year you are visible, and the forest counts you." Other names that stuck: "The oak has been waiting, the way a bank waits." "Cut a hundred years ago by someone who did not write it down." The game had a sense of growth and of something hidden, and it kept its promises.

## 4. What wasn't fun
1. **The floor going bare every few minutes (first ~2.5 hours).** Over and over: scroll down, tap MAX on leaf litter, scroll, tap MAX on needle mat. It was repetitive upkeep with no decision in it. Standing Order fixed it, but far too late.
2. **Fighting the interface.** The header keeps growing, so the area that scrolls ends up a narrow strip. Buttons sit hidden behind the header. Tapping near the log opens the full log sheet when I meant to buy something. When I scrolled through a bargain card, my thumb moved the TERM and VOLUME sliders: twice a contract jumped to term 4 without my noticing. Constant friction, and it made me nervous of the most interesting screen.
3. **Rules I couldn't read, and an ending that faded rather than closed.** I bought Dormancy Clause ("nothing defaults in the dark anymore"), put the phone down, and came back to "you did not deliver... it will say so to them". The birch's rep had dropped from 23 to 6. The game never told me why, and it felt unfair. "After food" and the coverage bar often contradicted the headline sugar rate. "Claim" on a patch didn't seem to cost anything, and the same patches showed up in two different places. Then, after Perennial Mycelium and Fruiting Body, the story simply ran out. The lines started repeating ("winter is not a pause", "roots do not find water", "you wrap a root...") with nothing new to discover.

## 5. The voice
Yes. The voice is the reason I played for nearly six hours. It's dry, exact and a little sorrowful, like nature writing told by an accountant who has read Ted Hughes. Lines that stuck:
- "you paid for something that was free an hour ago"
- "leaf litter changes hands. hands is a generous word here."
- "the price went up while you were buying. you did that."
- "a forest floor is not a floor. it is the top of something."
- "a tree ring is a year in which nothing happened fast enough to notice."
- "the elm is dying and knows the rate for it" / "the elm is finished. the collateral returns. nothing else does."
- "Monotropa has no chlorophyll and no shame. It takes sugar, buys trust, keeps the four percent."
- "the good years and the bad years are gone. there is only the average now, and it will not move."

I kept asking who is speaking. Is it the forest, a ledger, or the fungus narrating itself in the second person? I never found out, and I'd have liked one hint. Once the lines started to repeat, though, the voice lost a little of its spell.

## 6. How far I got
I grew from 0 g to 2.5 tonnes. I claimed all 6 patches and bought every adaptation, including Perennial Mycelium and Fruiting Body. The understory grew to nine trees: birch, aspen, two Douglas firs, hemlock, hollow beech, dying elm and oak. The biggest change came at about minute 7, when the bargain tab and the birch turned it from a tapping game into a game about trading with living things. A second change came at about minute 140, when patches, Standing Order and the bigger trees turned it from shopkeeping into a slow game of diplomacy with old trees. Minutes 210–250, with the beech, the ghost pipe and the elm, were where it felt most like Kentucky Route Zero. After Perennial Mycelium (~280) it became something smaller: a finished forest that keeps counting.

## 7. Would I keep playing tomorrow?
**Maybe, leaning no.** I'd open it to see whether "nothing to change yet" really means *yet*. But I stopped because the story stopped moving, and when I check, nothing seems to be waiting for me. If tomorrow brings a new voice or a new place, I'm back. If it's just bigger numbers, I'm not.

## 8. The one change that would make it more fun for me
Make the story go on after the last adaptation, and make the ending feel like an ending. After Perennial Mycelium and Fruiting Body, give me a final arc: whoever cut the coppice, what the floor is "the top of", who the voice is. Let it build to a close, or to a new place. Don't let it trail off into repeated lines and rotting sugar. A smaller, practical second wish: stop scrolling from moving the bargain sliders.
