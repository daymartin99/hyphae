# HYPHAE: Sam's playtest

## 1. Stop point
I stopped at **385 played minutes**, by my own choice. I didn't hit the cap or the tool limit. By then all six patches were claimed and the adaptation shop was empty except for Autolysis and Reflex Arc. I took Autolysis ("part of you dissolves on command now"), but I couldn't find any new control that used it. The Oak Rise card said "+oak", but no oak ever showed up in the understory. For the first time in seven hours I couldn't tell what the game wanted from me. All that was left was coming back, rebuying food and renewing contracts.

## 3. What was fun
1. **The birch contract (minutes 13-20).** The game had been about tapping and buying, and suddenly a 40-year-old birch would trade sugar for minerals. There were sliders for volume, term and collateral, and a coverage bar. It was discovery and optimising at once, and it's exactly my kind of game: a ledger with a tree on the other side. "the term ends. the birch remembers that it ended well" made me feel I'd built a relationship.
2. **The slow reveal of the economy.** First biomass. Then labile, as distinct from biomass. Then a sugar line that wasn't there before. Then minerals, market prices with a "fair" price once I bought the Ledger, seasons, cover and held water, patches. Every 10-20 minutes the screen grew a new line. That's the Paperclips feeling, a sense of growth and mystery. The drawing at the top went from one thread to a full fan, and the units went from g to kg to t. Both were small, quiet rewards.
3. **Cards that warn you and let you walk in anyway.** The Hollow Beech: "It is enormous and it is empty and it will pay almost anything. Read the term length twice." Forward Contracts: "when they turn against you, they stay turned." Perennial Mycelium: "nothing will ever beat the average again." This is anticipation and story. The upgrades read like small moral decisions, not stat bumps. I bought the beech knowing I was probably signing a bad lease, and that's why it was fun.

## 4. What wasn't fun
1. **Rebuying food every time I came back.** The tips strip the floor in seconds. Almost every return started with "the floor is bare" and sugar rotting at its cap. Standing Order "never sleeps", but it seemed to buy only while I was looking. That's repetition, waiting with nothing to decide, and a promise the game didn't keep.
2. **Taps landing in the wrong place.** Cards expand and the list jumps under my finger. The EXTEND button overlaps the bottom row of buy buttons. Sliders scroll the page if you drag them. Because of all that I declined offers I meant to keep, bought the wrong food, opened the wrong tree, and once spent 257 kg on tip MAX by accident. Default collateral also locked up 52-443 kg without my really noticing. That's friction, and it felt unfair because the mistakes weren't mine.
3. **The ending fizzled (minutes 350-385).** I finished the patches and bought out the shop. Then I held the one remaining "decision", and the log said I could now dissolve on command, but I found nothing to press. "+oak" never became an oak. Earlier the coverage bar was red for reasons I never understood, and an accepted offer vanished with "it needs more sugar behind it". At the end I felt lost and couldn't work out what the game wanted.

## 5. The voice
The voice is the best thing in the game and the reason I stuck with it. It's dry, exact and a little sad, and it keeps economics and ecology in the same sentence. Lines that stuck:
- "other things eat here. that is what a price is."
- "you paid for something that was free an hour ago"
- "paid for. the beetles were bidding too."
- "the price went up while you were buying. you did that."
- "a mushroom is not the organism. it is the part that is leaving."
- "you let it pass. there will be others, or there will not." (after I declined an offer)
- "the oak has been waiting, the way a bank waits."
- "the elm is dying and knows the rate for it."
- "claimed. the worms were here first and will be here after."
- "a tree ring is a year in which nothing happened fast enough to notice."

The log repeats itself a lot later on ("the floor is drying from the top down" must have shown up thirty times), which wears the voice thin.

## 6. How far I got
I reached 5.4 tonnes of biomass, 92+ hyphal tips, six patches, seven tree partners and six kinds of food (leaf to heartwood stump). Year 16, more or less.

The biggest change came around minute 130, after a 25-minute break. It stopped being a tapping and food-buying game and became a portfolio game. I was juggling contracts with several trees, reputation, collateral, mineral-gated patches and adaptations with real trade-offs. That stretch, minutes 130-230, is when it felt like a different and better game. After that it never changed shape again. It just got bigger until the shop ran out.

## 7. Would I keep playing tomorrow?
**Maybe.** I'd open it once to see whether the Hollow Beech's "long time" ever comes due, and whether Autolysis or the oak turns into something. If it's the same rebuy-and-renew routine, I'll stop. The writing earned the seven hours. The last hour didn't earn an eighth.

## 8. The one change that would make it more fun for me
Make the late game clearly point somewhere. When the shop empties, show the next goal, the fruiting body hinted at in "the part that is leaving", and make Autolysis visibly do something. As a close second, make Standing Order actually keep the floor stocked while I'm away, so coming back is about decisions and not chores.
