# HYPHAE: Sam's playtest

## 1. Stop point
I stopped at **417 played minutes**, which is the 420-minute cap. I didn't quit. I was in the game's second phase (the "MIND/FOREST" layer) and still wanted to see what it wanted from me. I'll admit the last half hour had started to drag. Every adaptation on the Mind tab cost more signal than my ceiling could hold (900 to 24,000 Σ against a 550 cap). The only way up was to colonise forest stands one at a time, then wait.

## 3. What was fun
1. **The phase change at about 328 minutes.** It started as "something is gathering. 12:35." I came back to a DECIDE card saying "it does not undo." I held it and the screen filled with STOP TASTING. The game came back with a new currency (signal Σ), FOREST and MIND tabs, and biomass jumping from 8 tonnes to 40, then 700, then kilotonnes. This is exactly why I play these games. It's the Universal Paperclips moment when the screen you've been optimising turns out to be the small one. *(Surprise, discovery, a sense of scale.)*
2. **Teases that pay off.** "At the edges, your threads are reaching for each other. They have not met." That line sat in the log for about an hour while I worked out that minerals were the gate. Then: "two threads met and did not stop at each other." Patch names worked the same way. The Windthrow Gap, the Old Coppice ("cut a hundred years ago by someone who did not write it down"), and the Oak Rise ("the oak has been waiting, the way a bank waits") each felt like the forest getting bigger. *(Anticipation, mystery, story.)*
3. **The economy as an accountant's toy.** It had tips that pay back, litter you can sell (with "it will be written down"), contracts with term, volume and collateral, Forward Contracts that lock prices "and when they turn against you, they stay turned", and the rot line on surplus sugar. When I noticed tips had become cheap relative to income and doubled them, growth jumped from about 70 g/s to over 1 kg/s. That felt like real optimising. *(Mastery, optimising, growth.)*

## 4. What wasn't fun
1. **Signing contracts in the Bargain tab.** Cards expand and collapse when you tap them. A new log line pushes the whole list down, so the SIGN button moves under your thumb. Twice I pressed SIGN and opened a different tree's card instead. The volume slider is also fiddly. It is the main money tab and it felt like filling in a form on a moving bus. *(Friction, unfairness.)*
2. **Not understanding the bargain numbers.** Reputation pips, "coverage" (red, amber, green), "exclusive", collateral that "the tree keeps if you default", and the Standing Order that seemed to buy back litter I had just sold. I never knew if a red coverage bar was dangerous or just suboptimal. I signed deals on faith. *(Confusion.)*
3. **The signal ceiling at the end.** Phase 2 opened with a dozen exciting cards, and every one said "more than you can hold." The game hinted ("a bigger vessel would keep it", "it needs more hollows"). It took me a session to connect that to advancing one stand at a time, with only one advance allowed at once. After the explosive switch, it went back to waiting with nothing to decide. *(Waiting, feeling lost.)*

## 5. The voice
Yes. It's the best thing in the game and the reason I stuck with the slow stretches. Lines that stuck:
- "The pressure has nowhere to go, so it becomes an opinion."
- "A hypha grows only at the tip. Everything behind the tip is plumbing."
- "A forest floor is not a floor. It is the top of something."
- "It cannot read you, the tree. It signs anyway."
- "Monotropa has no chlorophyll and no shame."
- "You stop having years. The average is very good, and nothing will ever beat the average again."
- "The price is higher than your ceiling. The ceiling can move."
- "Nothing above you owes you anything. It never did."

The voice also did real game design. It told me where I was blocked ("minerals come from the trees, under bargain") and it warned me before I bought something irreversible.

## 6. How far I got
The biggest change came at about **328 minutes**. Holding DECIDE after Action Potential turned a careful litter-and-sugar economy into a map of 61 named forest stands, with a Mind tree priced in signal and a rival "growing toward you from the north and it is not a tree." Before that the game changed steadily but within the same frame: Bargain at about 5 minutes, selling at about 53, more patches, more trees, more food types. At the end I had 14 kilotonnes of biomass, 3–4 of 61 stands, and 8 unspent "D" points I couldn't use yet.

## 7. Would I keep playing tomorrow?
**Yes.** The second phase is exactly the reveal I hoped for, and I want to find out what "consumed 0%" of the forest turns into, and what's coming from the north. I'd be nervous, though. If the Mind tab stays unaffordable for another session I'll start to feel it's arbitrary.

## 8. The one change that would make it more fun
Make the Bargain tab stop moving under my finger. Keep the tree cards and their SIGN buttons fixed while the log updates, and remember the last terms I used so I can re-sign with one tap. That's the money tab, and it's where most of my frustration came from.
