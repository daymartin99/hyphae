# HYPHAE: Robin's playtest

## 1. Stop point
**241 played minutes. I quit; I didn't hit the cap or the tool limit.** By the end I owned all six patches. The adaptations list said "nothing to change yet" and stayed that way after a 30-minute break. I had 5,000+ minerals and a full, rotting sugar store with nothing to spend them on, and the narrator had started repeating itself. The story stopped moving, and when that happens I stop.

## 2. Fun curve

**Average: 3.0**

## 3. What was fun
1. **The voice revealing the economy.** "you have been making this the whole time" when sugar appeared. "you paid for something that was free an hour ago" when I first had to buy litter. "the price went up while you were buying. you did that." Each new mechanic arrived as a small revelation instead of a tutorial. That is *discovery* and *story* together, and it's why I kept going.
2. **The trees as characters.** The birch "takes what you offer. it does not ask what you are." The hollow beech "is enormous and it is empty and it will pay almost anything. Read the term length twice." The elm "is dying and knows the rate for it." Ghost Pipe "has no chlorophyll and no shame". Each new name in the Understory felt like a new person walking into a play. That's *mystery* and *anticipation*: I wanted to know who would turn up next.
3. **The one real decision: "Sever the Elm".** It was labelled "(a decision)", it said "It was going to die anyway. (No penalty)", and it needed HOLD TO TAKE. It was the Disco Elysium moment I'd been hoping for: a choice with no numerical stakes, only moral ones. I didn't do it, and I felt something about not doing it. It was the only time the game asked who I was rather than how efficient I was.

## 4. What wasn't fun
1. **Buying Perennial Mycelium by accident.** The card list shifted as the sugar and food lines resized, and my tap landed on the wrong card. "the good years and the bad years are gone. there is only the average now, and it will not move." It's a beautiful line about an irreversible loss I never chose. It was *unfair* and *friction*, and it took away the seasons, which were part of the atmosphere I loved.
2. **The run-out at the end.** After the sixth patch there was nothing to buy. Minerals climbed to 5k with no use, sugar rotted at the cap, and the narrator cycled through lines I'd already read ("more is falling than anything can eat", "the thread finds a crack and follows it"). The "Oak Rise" promised "+oak" and "a patient partner", and the oak never appeared in my Understory. That's *waiting with nothing to decide* plus a broken promise in the story.
3. **Contract admin, and text that fought the numbers.** Every contract expired between sessions, so each evening began with opening every tree, dragging Volume, Term and Collateral, and scrolling to SIGN, sometimes for four or five trees. That's *repetition* and *friction*. Worse, the screen said "the tree sends down the first sweetness" while the numbers said I was paying *the tree* in sugar for minerals. I read every line, so the contradiction pulled me out. Accepting the birch's offered deal once simply failed with "there is not enough of you for that", and nothing told me why (*confusion*).

## 5. The voice
Yes, it was the whole reason I played for four hours. It's dry, exact and a little sad, and it knows what fungi actually do. Lines that stuck:
- "a mushroom is not the organism. it is the part that is leaving."
- "phosphorus. it left a rock forty years ago and a root this morning."
- "the aspen is not a tree. it is eighty trees with one root."
- "four of them are rooted within a metre of you. none arrived by accident."
- "a forest floor is not a floor. it is the top of something."
- "Cut a hundred years ago by someone who did not write it down."
- "The oak has been waiting, the way a bank waits."
- "the elm is dying and knows the rate for it."
- "the smell of rain on dry ground is something announcing it survived."

I kept asking who was speaking. Was it the fungus? The forest? Someone keeping accounts? Lines like "none arrived by accident" and "someone who did not write it down" promised an answer, or at least a deeper hint, and never delivered one. That unpaid mystery hurt more than any balance problem. The repeats also wore it down: a voice this good shouldn't say the same sentence five times in an evening.

## 6. How far I got
I went from 0 g of biomass to 1.6 t, from one patch to six, and from one birch to seven trees (birch, aspen, two Douglas firs, a hemlock, the hollow beech and a dying elm). I unlocked six foods, from leaf litter to heartwood stumps. The biggest change came around minute 7, when the floor went bare and I suddenly had to *buy* litter on a market with sugar. The tap game turned into an economy, and from about minute 11, a relationship with the trees through contracts. After that it never became something different again. It grew in size: more patches, more trees, bigger numbers. "Fruiting Body" sounded like the transformation the mushroom line had promised. It turned out to be +8 reputation.

## 7. Would I keep playing tomorrow?
**Maybe, leaning no.** "nothing to change yet" hints that more is coming, and I'd open it once to see whether the oak arrived or the voice had something new to say. But I left on a screen with nothing to decide and a pile of useless numbers. If the next session looks the same, I'm done. My friend was right that it's strange and literary. It ran out of story before it ran out of numbers.

## 8. The one change that would make it more fun for me
**Make the story move when the economy plateaus.** When I bought the last patch, it should have unlocked a real next chapter: the oak finally appearing, the fruiting body actually being "the part that is leaving" (a rebirth that keeps the forest's memory of me), and the narrator answering a little of "who is speaking". The writing already sets this up. The structure just needs to pay it off. (Close behind: stop the card list jumping under my finger, especially for irreversible purchases like Perennial Mycelium.)
